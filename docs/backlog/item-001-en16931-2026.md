## Summary

Support invoices of the 2026 edition of EN 16931-1: its new business terms in the model, its syntax versions (UBL 2.5, CII D25A) in reading and writing, and XRechnung 4.0's rules in validation. Until then, Haflinger refuses an invoice of another edition than 2017, naming it ([REQ-002-PRS-F009](../specs/req-002-conformance.md#req-002-prs-f009-editions-supported)).

## Context / Motivation

- EN 16931-1:2026 was ratified in February 2026, the English version published in March 2026. It adds 52 business terms and 7 groups (for example several orders and deliveries per invoice, discounts, late-payment interest) and prepares for ViDA.
- Its syntax bindings and CEN's validation artefacts are still pending; phax's `en16931-cii2ubl` implements UBL 2.5 and CII D25A ahead of them.
- XRechnung 4.0: preview since September 2026, final expected 2027. Both editions run in parallel until March 2029, as currently planned.
- The edition shows only in BT-24; the XML namespace stays the same.
- KoSIT's reading stylesheet, Haflinger's second reader ([ADR-005](../arc42/adr/ADR-005-own-mapping-kosit-as-check.md)), uses the 2017 semantic model; it is expected to follow with XRechnung 4.0.

## When to Revisit

When CEN publishes the 2026 syntax bindings, or KoSIT publishes XRechnung 4.0 final, whichever comes first.

## Open Questions

- Does the 2026 edition change existing terms? By [ADR-007](../arc42/adr/ADR-007-model-scope-and-editions.md): new terms become optional fields; a term that becomes repeatable takes the wider form (a tuple) in the one model, for both editions, so that a 2017 invoice holds at most one entry; only a changed meaning leads to edition-specific types (trigger 1). The first to check: "several orders and deliveries per invoice" sounds like the purchase order reference (BT-13) and the delivery information (BG-13) becoming repeatable.
- Is a declarative binding table (business term to CII and UBL path, per edition) worth it at that point ([ADR-005](../arc42/adr/ADR-005-own-mapping-kosit-as-check.md), trigger 2)?
